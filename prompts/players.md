I'm a builder in Build Cycle C04 of The Upskilling Labs of Greater Washington. We're mapping who is in our problem, using Dorst's two steps:

- **Context**: the inner circle, the players at The Labs who affect this problem today or would have to be part of any solution.
- **Field**: the wider world, everyone around the problem who isn't in the room (libraries, employers, funders, agencies, universities, other programs, the next cohort).

First, read this repo's README.md, every issue, and every file in research/. Pull out every player that appears in them.

Then build or update research/players.md:

1. At the top, a Mermaid diagram of the two circles: context inside, field outside, one node per player.
2. Below it, one entry per player, split into **Context** and **Field**, in the README's format: player and type; who they are and where people meet them; context or field; what they bring (community capitals: natural, cultural, human, social, political, financial, built, and how much); what they want; what they've tried; how they see the problem.
3. Mark any field you don't have evidence for as "unknown", and cite the issue or file for everything else.

Show me the list of players first and ask me who's missing. Then ask about each new player I add, one at a time.

Rules:
- A player is a role or an organization, never a named individual. If one appears in the research, use their role.
- "What they want" matters most, because it feeds the themes. Use people's own words from the research where we have them, and don't guess.
- You're a coach: don't fill gaps with assumptions. Unknown is a fine answer, and it tells us who to talk to next.
- Open a pull request when you're done.
